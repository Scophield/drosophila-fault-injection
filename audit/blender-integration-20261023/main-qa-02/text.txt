Paired Fault Assessment of a Fly-Connectome
Spiking Controller
Shuchao Gao
Waseda University
Tokyo, Japan
Abstract—Task-level fault loss in a spiking controller can
depend on feedback even when healthy success counts match.
We provide an auditable paired assessment with shared input
variates and deletion masks, causal feedback, exact scheduled
actuation and independent input/geometry replay. A 138,639-
neuron fly-connectome model is tested on 24 new seed clusters
crossing two laws, two bearings and three network conditions.
All 96 healthy episodes remain collision-free through one second.
At 50% connection-row deletion, proportional and relay control
succeed in 37/48 and 48/48 episodes; their paired loss difference
is 22.9 percentage points, with a 95% cluster interval of 12.5–
33.3 and Holm-adjusted p = 0.00195. The 25% contrast remains
uncertain. A frozen 400-ms command-rate criterion detects only
2/13 later collisions. Geometry-informed forecasting detects all
13 but flags 57/275 successful tasks. Task loss changes with the
declared horizon; stopped-duration matching leaves new group
activity medians unchanged. These findings support paired task
assessment without establishing biological or hardware fault
tolerance.
Index Terms—spiking neural networks, fault assessment, feed-
back control, connectome, neuromorphic systems
I. INTRODUCTION
An embedded spiking controller must act before contact.
A neural-rate criterion can accept a failed task, while altered
activity can accompany success. Assessment requires a defined
plant, causal actuation and a declared horizon.
Feedback couples faults to future sensory input. Equal seed
labels need not align inputs when stopping or event consump-
tion changes. Early termination also changes accumulated
spikes: stopped, common-duration and fixed-horizon counts
answer different resource questions. Pairing requires verified
healthy capability in every direction.
We couple a fly-connectome SNN to synthetic pursuit
avoidance. Assumed receptive fields and body coupling define
an engineering stress model, not biological escape. Separate
development and validation establish bilateral laws; new seed
clusters cross law, direction and nested deletion dose at one
second.
Our contribution is an executable paired protocol with
source-bound registrations, shared input variates and masks,
exact
actuation
and
independent
input/geometry
replay.
Prospective testing demonstrates feedback-dependent fault loss
despite identical healthy success counts, and quantifies neural
warning errors. Healthy reserves and actions differ: this is a
whole-system comparison. Standard statistics and kinematics
support bounded findings, not algorithmic novelty. Failed
development and all test cells are retained.
II. POSITIONING AND SCOPE
Shiu et al. [1] connect anatomical wiring to executable
sensorimotor hypotheses. An’s sparsification study [2] varies
connection strength, placement, state and task; Jo [3] re-
ports neural and virtual-arena responses to connectome dam-
age. These works already establish context- and endpoint-
dependent function. Our question concerns source-auditable
pairing, sampled-data actuation and same-record exposure
estimands in a prospective assessment, rather than a new claim
that endpoint choice matters.
SpikeFI [4] and SpikingJET [5] provide SNN injection
frameworks with application evaluation. NeuroBench [6] and
embodied-agent benchmarking [7] motivate separate func-
tional and resource measurements. Here the comparisons
rescore our own generated records; neither injector framework
is executed as a benchmark. Physical neutron tests [8] illustrate
the additional evidence needed to connect software fault mod-
els to hardware. Our weighted-row deletions have no calibrated
physical upset rate, power or area interpretation.

The first qualifying decision td schedules one 0.35 m/s

Success means collision-free through 1 s, not eventual es-

onds), so contact occurs by 0.8 s without a burst in this plant.
Failure latches at first contact; the trajectory and neural loop
continue for equal 1 s resource exposure. Post-contact activity
is counterfactual accounting, not physical collision dynamics
or warning data. Independent replay checks inputs, geometry,
decisions, clocks and horizon state without rerunning neural
dynamics.

IV. INDEPENDENT TEST RESULTS
A. Fault Loss Depends on Feedback
All 96 healthy core episodes succeed: 24/24 for each
law/bearing in Fig. 2. At 25% deletion, proportional control
succeeds in 46/48 episodes and relay in 48/48; at 50%, these
counts are 37/48 and 48/48. There are no healthy-to-fault
gains. The 50% proportional losses are direction-dependent:
10/24 at −30◦, 1/24 at +30◦. Both healthy directions remain
capable, avoiding the earlier directional floor.
The registered relay-minus-proportional net-loss contrast
at 50% is −22.9 percentage points (pp), 95% cluster in-
terval [−33.3, −12.5], with 11/24 nonzero cluster contrasts.
Symmetric-null sign-flip sensitivity gives raw p = 0.000977,
Holm-adjusted p = 0.00195. At 25%, the contrast is −4.2 pp,
interval [−10.4, 0], adjusted p = 0.50; two nonzero clusters
provide limited evidence. Relay’s zero losses are observations,
not zero-risk estimates. These new laws do not replicate the
older six-cluster original/gated comparison, whose exploratory
25% p = 0.25 remains in the supplement.
A post-hoc trace diagnostic finds identical trigger times
and pre-decision traces in all 144 matched law/scenario pairs.
Steering is gated below the trigger threshold, so the laws first
diverge after that decision. Median trigger time rises from 160
to 220 ms under 50% deletion for both laws; median absolute
heading at precontact 400 ms is 38.7◦for proportional control
and 96.0◦for relay. Healthy median minimum clearance is
33.1 versus 41.4 mm. Thus identical success counts do not
match safety reserve or executed turning effort. This describes
a timing/turning pathway, not mediation or fault-specific su-
periority independent of baseline margin.
B. Neural Criteria Miss Task Failures
All 400-ms assessments precede the earliest contact,
607.6 ms. Fig. 3 scores the same 288 records against 13
later collisions and 275 successful tasks. The command-rate
criterion detects only 2/13 collisions; its sensitivity interval
is [0, 40.0]%. Two-sided activity detects 3/13 with more false
alarms, and the upper activity limit detects none. At this frozen
calibration, adding a lower activity cutoff still misses 10/13
collisions.
Both geometry-informed forecasts detect 13/13 collisions.
Holding the current neural turn command reduces false alarms
from 84 to 57 relative to the same-state zero-future-turn base-
line. A secondary descriptive paired analysis gives a specificity
gain of 9.8 pp, interval [6.6, 13.4]. Its remaining 20.7% false-
alarm rate and exact-state information limit practical inter-
pretation. Zero observed misses yield a degenerate bootstrap
interval; the one-sided 95% bound on any miss in a new,
identically defined seed cluster is 11.7%, not a conditional
FNR guarantee. This candidate is not a validated protection
algorithm.
C. Prespecified Angle Transfer
Eight new ±45◦seed clusters retain the frozen map, laws
and thresholds. All 32 healthy episodes and all 32 25%-
deletion episodes succeed. At 50%, proportional control suc-
ceeds in 14/16 and relay in 16/16; both losses occur at negative
bearing. The relay-minus-proportional contrast is −12.5 pp,
interval [−31.3, 0]. This small stress cohort is not pooled with
the core and does not establish a precise replication. Of two
collisions, command rate flags one, two-sided activity none,

D. Separate Action-Interface Check
An additional eight-seed diagnostic was registered after
core launch and remains separate from its inference. On
64 new healthy-network episodes, normal proportional/relay
control each succeeds in 16/16; turning disabled with burst
triggering retained succeeds in 0/16, and both neural actions
disabled in 0/16. Baseline walking remains active. These
controls support action dependence in this plant; they do not
establish connectome-specific superiority or separately isolate
burst effects. The walk-only feasibility bound in Methods
explains why the latter failure is partly constructed.
E. Horizon and Exposure Have Distinct Effects
Only two collisions occur by 800 or 900 ms, versus 13
by 1 s. The 50% controller contrast is therefore −4.2 pp
at the earlier horizons, versus −22.9 pp at 1 s. These are
repeated observations, not extra samples; the declared horizon
materially changes task loss.
In contrast, duration matching leaves every group median
spike ratio unchanged. At 50% deletion, stopped/common
medians are 1.505 for proportional control and 1.257 for relay;
every pair changes by less than 0.00325. The large historical
stopped/common difference does not recur here. Proportional
50% activity has median ratio 0.981 at 400 ms and 1.505 over
1 s, illustrating distinct resource windows. Full-horizon post-
contact continuation is not prospective warning evidence.
V. EXECUTABLE EVIDENCE AND AUDIT COST
One JSON file per paired condition records frames and
actuator segments. Source, map, calibration and input bindings
reject incomplete or mixed matrices. Independent replay re-
constructs geometry, decisions and inputs without the task im-
plementation or connectome; arithmetic checks verify effects
and warning denominators. Mutation tests reject inconsistent
headings, bursts and horizon flags and future-data leakage in
prefix metrics. A tiny Brian 2 test checks state and fault-weight
restoration.
The core neural campaign takes 14,393 s with three CPU
workers; median episode wall time is 145.5 s. Independent
trace/input replay takes 34.4 s. These perform different work:
replay consumes recorded neural outputs and does not certify
neural-state correctness or establish a simulation speedup. No
hardware energy or area is inferred.
The package supplies raw JSON/CSV, generating snapshots,
local registrations, TeX, figures and checksums; external ta-
bles remain separate. Registrations lack external timestamps.
A logged CSV fix separates numeric-margin and Boolean-
warning columns; frozen sources and raw records remain
intact. Reanalysis/replay needs no neural execution. Public
main may predate this revision.
VI. IMPLICATIONS AND LIMITS
The same functional deletion mask can receive different
task-level fault labels under two healthy-capable feedback
laws. A neural threshold and a task requirement should there-
fore be reported separately, with actuator schedule, horizon
and healthy safety reserve attached. At the tested calibration,
a two-sided activity control also leaves substantial misses.
Evidence remains conditional on one v783 graph, one
assumed visual map and a simple finite-horizon pursuit task.
Healthy safety margins and turning effort are not matched;
the law contrast does not isolate fault-specific tolerance from
nominal reserve. The fixed rules are not optimized classifiers;
the twofold spike envelope is not an established resource
budget. Forecasts use more information than neural counts
and retain substantial false alarms. Sparse failure clusters
and fixed calibration limit uncertainty estimates. Row deletion
is uncalibrated to physical bit errors; energy, area, real-time
execution and biological tolerance are not established.
VII. CONCLUSION
An
auditable
paired
assessment
separates
feedback-
dependent task loss from neural-rate and activity criteria in a
large connectome SNN. Independent evidence supports a 50%
deletion contrast at the specified one-second endpoint; the 25%
contrast remains uncertain and stopped-duration matching has


little effect in the new cohort. The reusable result is a source-
bound assessment and replay workflow with explicit task,
timing and resource endpoints.

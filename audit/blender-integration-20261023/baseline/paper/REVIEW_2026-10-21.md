**Recommendation: 3/10 - reject in its current form; confidence: 4/5.** Separating transmission, activity compliance and behavior is useful. Corrected hub experiments remain unperformed, and six calibration episodes are included in the reported healthy evaluation.

## Phase 1 - Understand the Paper

Scope: I read `paper/main.tex`, all five included sections, both bibliographies, the IEEEtran class/style configuration, reproduction and repair records, and relevant simulation/scoring code. I inspected all five PDF pages and recomputed archive statistics. No cover/response letter or manuscript appendix was found. Locations below use filenames under `paper/sections/` unless otherwise specified.

1. **Problem.** How do hardware-motivated perturbations affect transmission, activity and synthetic escape behavior in a fixed whole-connectome fly-brain spiking model?

2. **Claimed contributions** (`intro.tex:7`), followed by my interpretation:

   - "a hardware-motivated fault-injection framework": five software perturbation families applied to an existing model.
   - "archived tolerance curves": conditional descriptive evidence at the principal 150 Hz operating point.
   - "pathway-mass controls suggesting compressive recruitment": a proposed explanation, without causal identification.
   - "five provisional design implications": engineering hypotheses requiring a hardware mapping or broader validation.
   - "a synthetic closed loop": an illustration that transmission, activity and task outcome differ; evaluation independence and environment validity need repair.

3. **Method.** A Brian2 LIF network instantiates 138,639 neurons and 15,091,983 connections representing 54.5 million contacts. Poisson input drives 104 LC4 and 210 LPLC2 neurons; eight descending neurons provide the command readout. Five perturbation families modify weights, connections, unit outputs, hub outputs or sensory outputs. Controls comprise target permutation, unsigned walk mass and inhibitory/composition-matched lesions. A calibrated command difference steers a planar agent with a threshold-triggered burst.

4. **Experimental setup.** There is one biological connectome and one archived target-permuted graph, with 19 conditions per main phase and 12 trials of 600 ms per condition. F1 uses lognormal sigma 0.05/0.10/0.20/0.50; F2 deletes 5/10/25/50%; F3 silences 1/2/5/10% of eligible unit outputs; F4 targets 1/2/5% hubs; F5 silences either/both sensory channels. Parameters include threshold -45 mV, reset -52 mV, membrane constant 20 ms, synaptic constant 5 ms, refractory period 2.2 ms and delay 1.8 ms. Main input is 150 Hz, with selected 75/300 Hz controls. Metrics are command rate, first-spike latency, total spikes, threshold passes, escape and paired activity ratios. Open-loop threshold is 51.4 Hz; demo threshold is 25.625 Hz. Fault seeds derive from SHA-256; open-loop Poisson streams are not explicitly reseeded. Healthy behavioral evaluation uses four azimuths and seeds 0-5; fault evaluation uses seeds 0-2. Dispersion bars are 10th-90th percentiles; median CIs use 20,000 bootstrap resamples, seed 0. No formal hypothesis tests remain. Runtime is approximately 3.5 seconds per standard-engine trial; CPU model, full compute accounting and a validated simulator environment are missing.

## Phase 4 - Detailed Findings

### Blocker Issues

**B1. Corrected code has not validated the hub conclusions.** Locations: `results.tex:23`, `principles.tex:8`; `REPAIR_REPORT.md:67`.

The manuscript states: "Matched reruns are still required: archived hub responses do not validate the corrected selector." Reconstruction finds three protected command cells in the old 1% set; the inhibitory/excitatory split changes from 765/621 to 766/620. Backend and selected-set differences also confound historical activity comparisons. A caveat cannot support the recommendation to protect inhibitory hubs.

**Fix:** rerun healthy, F4, HUB_inh/HUB_exc, composition and random-inhibitory controls using identical selector manifests, backend and controlled input streams. Recompute the expected-mass control's target size. Report realized cells, edges and contacts, and uncertainty on contrasts. Otherwise remove hub-specific design conclusions.

**B2. The 24/24 healthy result includes training episodes.** Location: `results.tex:42`; `sim_vision/calibrate_readout.py:18`.

All six negative-azimuth seed-0/1/2 training logs exactly equal their counterparts in `episodes_v4`. Those episodes determine the takeoff threshold and also enter 24/24 evaluation. Thus this total is not wholly held out. The 12 positive-azimuth episodes remain a distinct evaluation subset; overlap does not invalidate every behavioral observation.

**Fix:** label the six reused episodes as calibration, report the remaining evaluation separately, then freeze calibration and evaluate new seeds across both azimuth signs. Document the initial controller used to generate calibration trajectories and separate controller selection from final testing.

**B3. Behavioral validity depends on underreported environment and stopping rules.** Location: `results.tex:42`; `sim_vision/run_closedloop.py:75`.

The looming envelope comes from elapsed time rather than actual separation (line 80). Adding heading back into relative bearing cancels orientation before receptive-field encoding (lines 78-89). Furthermore, assigning a new horizon inside an already-created `range` does not extend execution (lines 145-146). Eight healthy episodes stop while still bursting. F2_50 episodes end after 30-37 frames versus 50 for nonfrontal healthy episodes, making total-spike ratios sensitive to exposure.

**Fix:** specify world/body coordinate frames, derive retinal input from actual geometry for an embodied claim, and define collision-free survival over an explicit horizon. Repair or remove the ineffective extension. Report common-window spike rates alongside episode totals, and test horizon, threat speed, burst delay and receptive-field sensitivity.

**B4. The topology control does not identify the proposed mechanism.** Locations: `methods.tex:19`, `results.tex:28`, Fig. 3a.

The null already fails without damage, so it cannot establish superior *fault tolerance* relative to a functioning alternative. The paper also discloses incorrect archived null deletion sampling before sparse-edge coalescing. Comparing unsigned all-DN walk mass with eight-cell output is not a decomposition of topology and dynamics.

**Fix:** regenerate graph curves from original-row deletion samples; add multiple rewiring realizations and a baseline-matched functional control. Examine signed input, recruitment and input-output gain, or retain only descriptive retention results.

**B5. Hardware implications exceed the demonstrated fault model.** Locations: `methods.tex:16`, `principles.tex:3`.

Positive lognormal scaling is neither bit flipping nor quantization; silencing neuronal outputs while neurons continue spiking is not an implemented dead-core fault. Real mappings can group many neurons and share routing resources.

**Fix:** add one documented hardware mapping with a realistic quantization, stuck-at or correlated routing/core perturbation, and quantify its effect on the three endpoints. A simulation-only paper is acceptable, but its hardware relevance must be explicit and testable. Compare the hand rule or another task controller under analogous sensor/actuator disturbances.

### Equations and Proofs

There are no numbered equations, theorems or appendix proofs. The nontrivial mathematical claims need these corrections:

- **Pathway mass:** `methods.tex:19` must define a nonnegative adjacency with target-row/source-column convention. The implementation constructs source rows and applies `Af.T`, using absolute weights (`sim/pathway_analysis.py:28`). State explicitly that mass counts unsigned weighted **walks**, including repeats, with weight-dependent units.
- **Deletion scaling:** for independent edge survival, the exact expectation is
  `E[m_k] = sum_w a_w p^{d(w)}`,
  where `d(w)` counts distinct edges in a walk. The simpler `p^k` requires no repeated edges, or an approximation argument. Neither establishes a nonlinear recruitment mechanism.
- **Numerical checks:** F2_50 retention is 0.764576 and latency ratio is 5.4/4.05 = 1.3333. The four reported retention/survival ratios are correct. For F1, `exp(sigma Z - sigma^2/2)` is mean preserving; sigma=0.5 implies coefficient of variation 53.3%, so "50% weight variability" needs definition.
- **Operational envelope:** state the inequalities before Results and justify sensitivity to the multiplier. A twofold spike threshold is not an energy or safety theorem.

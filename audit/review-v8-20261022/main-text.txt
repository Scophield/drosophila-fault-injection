Shuchao Gao

Abstract—Fault assessment can conflate preserved neural
transmission with task tolerance when cohorts, exposure and
control laws differ. We quantify these dependencies in a 138,639-
neuron Drosophila spiking model. A frozen-calibration compar-
ison crosses two laws with healthy, 25%-deletion and 50%-
deletion networks on six paired seed clusters, comprising 72
records. Collision-free counts are 4/4/0 of 12 under the original
law and 6/3/0 under pre-cue gating. Gated-minus-original net
fault loss is +25.0 and +16.7 percentage points at the two
doses. The 25% conditional percentile range excludes zero, but
only three clusters have nonzero contrasts; a post-hoc sign-flip
sensitivity gives p = 0.25. All factorial +30◦episodes collide.
Archive reanalysis removes 12 of 14 additional rejections relative
to transmission-only scoring when targeted hubs are excluded.
Duration matching changes the median deletion spike ratio from
0.382 to 0.729; six equal-duration hub pairs instead cross twofold
at 840–900 ms on calibration scenarios. These conditional findings
support reporting cohorts, paired exposure, analysis horizons and
task outcomes together, without establishing a resource budget
or broad reliability.
Index Terms—neuromorphic computing, connectome, fault
assessment, fault injection, Drosophila
I. INTRODUCTION
Fault tolerance inferred from a neural endpoint can depend
on which fault cohorts, observation durations and feedback
laws enter the assessment. Preserved command transmission
can coexist with elevated activity or failed behavior. The adult
Drosophila wiring diagram [1], [2] and an executable spiking
model [3] provide an anatomically constrained substrate for
quantifying this dependence. Biological wiring supplies a
structural prior without validating a simplified controller’s
reliability.
We study software perturbations of a 138,639-neuron leaky
integrate-and-fire (LIF) model, driving LC4/LPLC2 visual
neurons and reading eight descending neurons (Fig. 1). Five
families alter weights, delete weighted connection rows or
silence neuronal outputs. These experiments specify neither
hardware-resource mapping nor biological resilience.
The contribution is empirical: we quantify how assessment
choices change the interpretation of fault effects in this model.
Archive reanalysis holds observations fixed while varying
cohort and exposure definitions; prospective tests cross two
frozen control laws with healthy, 25%-deletion and 50%-
deletion networks. Paired task loss separates fault effects
from baseline controller success. Duration matching, complete

II. RELATED WORK
Connectome models connect anatomy to executable sen-
sorimotor hypotheses [3], while graph statistics characterize
wiring rather than dynamical reliability [2]. A recent spar-
sification study investigates compression across connection
strength, placement, state and task, including active-state over-
excitation [4]. Endpoint-dependent damage responses have
also been reported on the male central nervous system connec-
tome [5]. Those studies investigate how damage or sparsifica-
tion changes function. Our assessment comparison explicitly
separates paired exposure, full observation horizons and neural
versus task endpoints. It introduces no perturbation operator
or claim of superior fault tolerance. Bilateral integration mea-
sured in the giant-fiber circuit [6] cautions against treating the
controller’s directional failure as established fly behavior.
SpikeFI [7], SpikingJET [8] and enpheeph [9] provide
SNN fault-injection frameworks. Population-coded regression

III. MODEL, PERTURBATIONS AND ENDPOINTS
Evidence is tiered: prospective geometry-corrected experi-
ments provide the principal task evidence; archive reanalysis
supplies cohort/exposure sensitivity; reconstructed historical
selectors are diagnostic only. Missing historical metadata are
not inferred from current code.
A. Spiking Substrate and Readout
The model uses the released signed connectivity and param-
eters [3], implemented with Brian 2 [15]. There are 138,639
neurons and 15,091,983 connection-table rows representing
54.5 million contacts. Between events, each unit follows

Independent 150 Hz Poisson trains drive 104 LC4 and 210
LPLC2 cells (Fig. 1). Each external event adds 68.75 mV to
the target voltage. Other cells receive no background drive.
LC4/LPLC2 are associated with looming processing, includ-
ing measured LPLC2 motion opponency [16]; the demo’s
receptive fields are assumptions, not measured maps. The
command readout is eight cells of types DNp01, DNa02,
DNp04 and DNb01 [17]. For duration T = 0.6 s, command
rate is r = Ncmd/(8T). Latency is the first spike in this group,
not a command decision time. Total activity S counts all model
spikes.
B. Functional Perturbations
F1 multiplies each weight by exp(σZ −σ2/2), Z
∼
N(0, 1), with σ = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.50. This preserves ex-
pected weight; it does not implement bit flips. F2 indepen-
dently zeroes weighted connection-table rows with proba-
bilities f = 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, not individual contacts;
expected lost contact mass is f. F3 silences all outgoing
connections of a random fraction 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10 of
eligible units, protecting sensory and command cells. Those
units still receive input and spike. F4 targets the top 1%, 2%
or 5% by outgoing contact count; its archived selections lack
cell manifests and have protection inconsistencies. F5 silences
LC4, LPLC2 or both output channels.
There are 12 fault-seeded 600 ms trials per condition. F1–F3
draw realizations from SHA-256 seeds over (phase, condition,
trial). Archived open-loop stimulus streams advance without
explicit per-trial reseeding; dispersion combines fault and stim-
ulus variation. Fresh code saves selected cell identities, input
hashes and backend metadata. Corrected selection excludes
protected FlyWire identities and breaks ties by identity. It has
not generated the historical F4 data.

C. Transmission Under Five Perturbation Families
Healthy command rate is 94.69 Hz (95% median CI 94.06–
95.42), with first-spike latency 4.05 ms. Equal-sized random
drive yields approximately 8.2 Hz despite greater activity; the
intact target-permuted graph yields zero. This specificity does
not isolate a fault-tolerance mechanism.
The supplement supplies every main dose, recorded control
damage and selected-dose intervals. F1 at σ = 0.50 retained
99.4% of median rate, with 10th–90th percentiles 90.40–
100.81 Hz versus healthy 93.96–95.63 Hz. F3 at 10% retained
100.4%. These sampled medians do not establish equivalence.
F2 retained 97.0%, 98.3%, 89.2% and 76.5% of median
command rate at 5%, 10%, 25% and 50% deletion. The 95%
bootstrap interval for the ratio of median rates at 50% was
75.1–79.0%. Latency rose from 4.05 to 5.40 ms (ratio 1.33,
interval 1.25–1.40). All sampled deletion trials passed trans-
mission; a separate 75 Hz control passed only 10/12 at 50%
deletion. F5 retained 79.8% or 67.9% when removing LC4
or LPLC2 outputs; silencing both eliminated transmission.
Supplementary plots show all doses and dispersion.
Twelve new 600 ms runs tested the sensory refractory
variant on three paired seeds. Median paired 50%-deletion
retention was 76.3% at 2.2 ms versus 79.2% at zero sensory
refractory time. Healthy median rates were 94.8 and 103.1 Hz.
These conditional observations do not establish variant invari-
ance.

E. Legacy Evaluation and Stopping-Time Matching
Legacy looming used elapsed time and heading canceled
from receptive-field bearing. Six threshold-calibration logs
duplicate healthy evaluation records; all fault episodes share
calibration seeds. Four-bearing/three-seed survival was 10/12
for 25% deletion, 0/12 for 50%, 12/12 for full hubs and 6/12
for inhibitory hubs. Eight healthy records stopped during a
burst. These are compromised recorded-horizon illustrations,
not corrected geometric tests (Supplementary Table S5).
For legacy 50% deletion, early collisions shorten fault
records relative to healthy references. Matching each pair to
T∗changes the median spike ratio from Rwhole = 0.382 to
Rcommon = 0.729. Fixed 200–500 ms ratios span 0.723–0.745.
This stopping-time mismatch is distinct from the horizon effect
below; neither ratio defines an activity budget.
F. Late Activity Under Equal Durations
All full-hub and paired healthy records last 1000 ms, so
Rwhole = Rcommon for each pair. No ratio exceeds twofold
through 800 ms. Six first crossings occur at 840–900 ms, all
at −60◦or −30◦on calibration seeds (Fig. 3). Thus selecting
400 ms excludes late exceedances; it does not remove unequal
exposure. Pooled medians rise from 1.362 at 400 ms to 1.703
at 1000 ms. Inhibitory hubs exceed twofold in every pair at all
200–500 ms windows. Individual trajectories and three-seed
sensitivity are supplied; sparse conditional bootstrap ranges
do not establish independent reliability.

V. IMPLICATIONS, LIMITATIONS AND CONCLUSION
Fault comparisons require declared cohorts, criteria and
stopping rules. Targeted-hub exclusion leaves two disagree-
ments; the main non-hub sweep shows none. Duration match-
ing changes deletion ratios, whereas early hub horizons omit
late activity. The six recorded one-second exceedances remain
after duration matching. Resource-failure prediction requires
independent budgets and held-out healthy false-alarm mea-
surements.
One connectome, selected drives and small samples limit
transfer. The intact null is nonfunctional; missing hub mani-
fests and compromised legacy evaluation limit interpretation.
Post-hoc exclusions and windows require prospective confir-
mation. Software perturbations support no hardware protection
policy without mapped resources and faults.
The new factorial comparison directly pairs faults within
each frozen control law. Gating improves healthy counts from
4/12 to 6/12, yet at 25% deletion yields 3/12 versus 4/12. The
original law’s zero net loss at this dose contains one loss and
one gain, not absence of fault effects. The positive conditional
interaction range rests on three nonzero seed clusters; post-
hoc sign-flip sensitivity does not supply confirmatory evidence.
At 50% deletion both laws fail all 12 episodes, so their net-
loss difference reflects healthy baseline success. Six clusters,
directional floors, negative/frontal calibration coverage and
assumed receptive fields limit generalization. Some survivors
are still bursting at 1 s; bounded survival does not establish
eventual escape.
Report transmission, activity and bounded task outcomes
with cohort denominators, paired exposure, analysis horizon,
termination reason and direction-specific results. Retain paired
losses and gains as well as net effects. A pass fraction cannot
certify reliability or a safety budget.

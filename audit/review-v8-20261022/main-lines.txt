32: of a Fly-Connectome Spiking Model}
33: 
34: \author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Shuchao Gao}
35: \IEEEauthorblockA{Waseda University\\
36: Tokyo, Japan}}
37: 
38: \maketitle
39: \bstctlcite{IEEEcontrol}

41: \begin{abstract}
42: Fault assessment can conflate preserved neural transmission with task tolerance when cohorts, exposure and control laws differ. We quantify these dependencies in a 138{,}639-neuron \emph{Drosophila} spiking model. A frozen-calibration comparison crosses two laws with healthy, 25\%-deletion and 50\%-deletion networks on six paired seed clusters, comprising 72 records. Collision-free counts are 4/4/0 of 12 under the original law and 6/3/0 under pre-cue gating. Gated-minus-original net fault loss is +25.0 and +16.7 percentage points at the two doses. The 25\% conditional percentile range excludes zero, but only three clusters have nonzero contrasts; a post-hoc sign-flip sensitivity gives $p=0.25$. All factorial $+30^\circ$ episodes collide. Archive reanalysis removes 12 of 14 additional rejections relative to transmission-only scoring when targeted hubs are excluded. Duration matching changes the median deletion spike ratio from 0.382 to 0.729; six equal-duration hub pairs instead cross twofold at 840--900\,ms on calibration scenarios. These conditional findings support reporting cohorts, paired exposure, analysis horizons and task outcomes together, without establishing a resource budget or broad reliability.
43: \end{abstract}

Paired Fault Assessment of a Fly-Connectome
Spiking Controller
Shuchao Gao
Waseda University
Tokyo, Japan
Abstract—Task-level fault loss in a spiking controller can
depend on feedback even when healthy performance matches.
We provide an auditable paired assessment with shared input
variates and deletion masks, causal feedback, exact scheduled
actuation and independent input/geometry replay. A 138,639-
neuron fly-connectome model is tested on 24 new seed clusters
crossing two laws, two bearings and three network conditions.
All 96 healthy episodes remain collision-free through one second.
At 50% connection-row deletion, proportional and relay control
succeed in 37/48 and 48/48 episodes; their paired loss difference
is 22.9 percentage points, with a 95% cluster interval of 12.5–
33.3 and Holm-adjusted p = 0.00195. The 25% contrast remains
uncertain. A frozen 400-ms command-rate criterion detects only
2/13 later collisions. Geometry-informed forecasting detects all
13 but flags 57/275 successful tasks. Task loss changes with the
declared horizon; stopped-duration matching leaves new group
activity medians unchanged. These findings support paired task
assessment without establishing biological or hardware fault
tolerance.
Index Terms—spiking neural networks, fault assessment, feed-
back control, connectome, neuromorphic systems
I. INTRODUCTION
An embedded spiking controller must produce an effective
action before contact. A neural-rate criterion can therefore
accept an episode that fails its task, while altered network
activity can accompany successful behavior. Assessment re-
quires a defined plant, a causal neural-to-actuator interface and
a declared observation horizon.
Closed-loop comparisons also couple faults to future sen-
sory input. Equal seed labels alone need not align random
inputs when stopping or event consumption changes. Early ter-
mination changes the interval over which spikes are accumu-
lated. Stopped-episode, common-duration and fixed-horizon
counts answer different resource questions. Their relationship
to task failure should be measured on paired records after
verifying healthy capability in every direction.
We couple a fly-connectome spiking substrate to a synthetic
pursuit-avoidance task. Its assumed receptive fields and body
coupling define an engineering stress model, not biological
escape. Separate development and validation establish two
bilateral laws; new seed clusters cross law, direction and nested
deletion dose at a fixed one-second endpoint.
Our engineering contribution is an executable paired proto-
col with source-bound registrations, shared input variates and
masks, exact actuation and independent input/geometry replay.
The prospective experiment demonstrates controller-dependent
fault loss despite matched healthy task success, and quantifies

II. POSITIONING AND SCOPE
Shiu et al. [1] connect anatomical wiring to executable
sensorimotor hypotheses. An’s sparsification study [2] varies
connection strength, placement, state and task; Jo [3] re-
ports neural and virtual-arena responses to connectome dam-
age. These works already establish context- and endpoint-
dependent function. Our question concerns source-auditable
pairing, sampled-data actuation and same-record exposure
estimands in a prospective assessment, rather than a new claim
that endpoint choice matters.
SpikeFI [4] and SpikingJET [5] provide SNN injection
frameworks with application evaluation. NeuroBench [6] and
embodied-agent benchmarking [7] motivate separate func-
tional and resource measurements. Here the comparisons
rescore our own generated records; neither injector framework
is executed as a benchmark. Physical neutron tests [8] illustrate
the additional evidence needed to connect software fault mod-
els to hardware. Our weighted-row deletions have no calibrated
physical upset rate, power or area interpretation.

D. Validation, Warnings and Inference
An initial controller failed its declared capability screen;
all attempts are retained. Final calibration passed on eight
new healthy validation clusters, 8/8 per law/bearing. A two-
cluster fault screen was developmental. Core testing uses 24
new clusters (288 episodes); eight separate ±45◦clusters test
angle transfer. Parameters, thresholds and stopping rules were
frozen before both tests.
Warnings use only data through 400 ms: low mean com-

law/side-specific healthy medians. A held-command forecast
uses current geometry, neural turn command and pending
burst; absent a trigger it assumes the earliest next-frame
decision. A same-state actuator baseline sets future turn to
zero. Both have exact simulated geometry, not implemented
sensors or reachability guarantees. Lower fifth-percentile com-

All test records contribute sensitivity, specificity, false
alarms and misses against contact through 1 s. Confidence

800/900/1000 ms task horizons are repeated observations, not
new replicates. No test tuning, seed replacement or efficacy
stopping is permitted. Inference remains conditional on one
model, map and task.
IV. INDEPENDENT TEST RESULTS
A. Fault Loss Depends on Feedback
All 96 healthy core episodes succeed: 24/24 for each
law/bearing in Fig. 2. At 25% deletion, proportional control
succeeds in 46/48 episodes and relay in 48/48; at 50%, these
counts are 37/48 and 48/48. There are no healthy-to-fault
gains. The 50% proportional losses are direction-dependent:
10/24 at −30◦, 1/24 at +30◦. Both healthy directions remain
capable, avoiding the earlier directional floor.
The registered relay-minus-proportional net-loss contrast
at 50% is −22.9 percentage points (pp), 95% cluster in-
terval [−33.3, −12.5], with 11/24 nonzero cluster contrasts.

A post-hoc trace diagnostic finds identical trigger times
and pre-decision traces in all 144 matched law/scenario pairs.
Steering is gated below the trigger threshold, so the laws first
diverge after that decision. Median trigger time rises from
160 to 220 ms under 50% deletion for both laws; median
absolute heading at the precontact 400-ms window is 38.7◦
for proportional control and 96.0◦for relay. This describes
a timing/turning pathway, not an identified mediation effect.
Maximum turn rates match; executed turning effort does not.
The result concerns feedback context rather than an intrinsi-
cally more fault-tolerant network.
B. Neural Criteria Miss Task Failures
All 400-ms assessments precede the earliest contact,
607.6 ms. Table I scores the same 288 records against 13
later collisions and 275 successful tasks. The command-rate
criterion detects only 2/13 collisions; its sensitivity interval
is [0, 40.0]%. Two-sided activity detects 3/13 with more false
alarms, and the upper activity limit detects none. Thus, simply
adding a lower activity cutoff does not resolve the endpoint
mismatch.
Both geometry-informed forecasts detect 13/13 collisions.
Holding the current neural turn command reduces false alarms
from 84 to 57 relative to the same-state zero-future-turn base-
line. A secondary descriptive paired analysis gives a specificity
gain of 9.8 pp, interval [6.6, 13.4]. Its remaining 20.7% false-
alarm rate and exact-state information limit practical inter-
pretation. Zero observed misses yield a degenerate bootstrap
interval; the one-sided 95% bound on any miss in a new,
identically defined seed cluster is 11.7%, not a conditional
FNR guarantee. This candidate is not a validated protection
algorithm.
C. Horizon and Exposure Have Distinct Effects
Only two collisions occur by 800 or 900 ms, versus 13
by 1 s. The 50% controller contrast is therefore −4.2 pp
at the earlier horizons, versus −22.9 pp at 1 s. These are
repeated observations, not extra samples; the declared horizon
materially changes task loss.
In contrast, duration matching leaves every group median
spike ratio unchanged. At 50% deletion, stopped/common
medians are 1.505 for proportional control and 1.257 for relay;
the maximum individual change over all pairs is 0.00325.
The large historical stopped/common difference does not recur
here. Proportional 50% activity has median ratio 0.981 at
400 ms and 1.505 over 1 s, illustrating distinct resource win-
dows. Full-horizon post-contact continuation is not prospective
warning evidence.
V. IMPLICATIONS AND LIMITS
The same functional deletion mask can receive different
task-level fault labels under two healthy-capable feedback
laws. A neural threshold and a task requirement should there-
fore be reported as separate specifications, with the actuator
schedule and task horizon attached. The two-sided activity
control shows that the discrepancy is not explained solely by
choosing an upper envelope for a deletion fault.
Evidence remains conditional on one v783 graph, one as-
sumed visual map and a simple finite-horizon pursuit task. The
laws share actuator limits but use different turning histories;
relay performance does not establish lower control cost or a
hardware protection policy. Geometry-informed forecasts have
more information than neural counts, and false alarms remain
substantial. Sparse failure clusters and fixed calibration limit
uncertainty estimates. Functional row deletion is uncalibrated
to physical bit errors, and no energy, area, real-time execution
or biological fault tolerance is established.
VI. CONCLUSION
An

timing and resource endpoints.

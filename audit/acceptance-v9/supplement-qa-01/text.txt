Assessment v9: matched source, data and protocol
This document separates the independent core test, a prespecified angle stress test, development, and historical audits.
Every new table is reconstructed from saved raw records and frozen registrations. Historical observations are retained
with their original limitations; they are not silently relabeled as new experiments.
1
Protocol, software and calibration
The current task is task-v9.1-causal-exact-events. The main Methods and paper/PROTOCOL_v9.md define the
sampled plant, common-random-number coupling, first-passage task outcome and resource estimands. All test pa-
rameters were frozen before outcomes. The neural substrate uses released FlyWire v783 tables and Shiu parameters;
the original publication used v630. The synthetic visual/body coupling is not validated fly behavior. Functional row
deletions are not physical bit faults. Between spikes the voltage and voltage-valued synaptic drive obey
˙vi = (v0 −vi + gi)/(20 ms),
˙gi = −gi/(5 ms).
Recurrent events add signed contact counts times 0.275 mV to gi after 1.8 ms. At threshold −45 mV, reset sets vi to
v0 = −52 mV and gi to zero; both equations pause during 2.2 ms refractoriness. Sensory cells retain this refractoriness,
a variant of upstream stimulation. External events add 68.75 mV to voltage; all non-sensory external rates are zero.
Initial neural states, monitors and unmodified recurrent weights are restored before each episode. A separate tiny
actual Brian2 test verifies this reset, including reapplying external weights, without loading a full connectome.

The originally frozen analyzer passed the complete input/source/timing checks but then failed on duplicate numeric-
margin/Boolean-warning CSV keys, before reporting statistics. The original script, failure log and output directory are
retained. The documented exportfix analyzer renames only numeric CSV fields, preserving all numerical definitions,
thresholds, source guards and raw records; canonical analyses are core-v2 and stress-v2. This is a post-registration
analytical export correction, not a new hypothesis or neural rerun. See paper/PROTOCOL_v9_NOTES.md.
A post-hoc control diagnostic verifies identical first trigger times and pre-decision traces in all 144 paired law/scenario
cells. Both laws gate steering below the trigger threshold, so their first action divergence follows that same decision.
Table entries at 400 ms precede every contact. Equal maximum turn rate does not match executed turning effort;
these diagnostics are not causal mediation estimates or energy measurements.

All prospective warnings use data through 400 ms, verified before first contact in every record. The two-sided activity
score is the absolute log count ratio to the law/side-specific validation median. Its cutoff is the healthy 95th percentile;
command and forecast cutoffs are fifth percentiles. The same nominal 5% healthy flag calibration is not a guarantee of
task FPR. Held-command and schedule forecasts share exact simulated geometry and the pending actuator schedule;
the candidate holds the current neural-derived turn command, the baseline sets future turn to zero. If no trigger
exists, both assume the earliest next-frame decision. This is an optimistic extrapolation, not a neural-only detector
or reachability proof.

Full conditional cluster intervals for sensitivity, specificity, FPR and FNR are in the source summary JSON. Resam-
pling keeps every law/bearing/dose within seed together. Zero observed errors may produce a degenerate bootstrap
interval; the one-sided 95% bound for the probability of at least one error in an otherwise identically defined seed
cluster is 1 −0.051/n. This is a cluster-any-error bound, not a conditional FPR/FNR guarantee. No population or
hardware zero-risk claim follows.
The original registration gives an approximate 0.20 planning half-width without stating its range explicitly. This
normal maximum-variance approximation applies only to [0, 1] success/loss/gain cluster means. Signed net loss spans
[−1, 1], and its between-law contrast can span [−2, 2], yielding range-based planning approximations about 0.40 and
0.80, respectively. These are not finite-sample coverage guarantees. The clarification in paper/PROTOCOL_v9_NOTES.
md preserves the registration bytes, sample size and analysis. Actual intervals are reported; small interactions and
equivalence are not established by the sample size.

Separate motor-interface ablations
Baseline walking remains 0.05 m/s when neural actions are disabled; the agent is not immobilized. Burst necessity
follows from this toy plant by construction.
For a walk-only segment of duration ∆t ≤0.02 s with precontact
separation d ≥0.05 m, the pursuer moves 0.3∆t toward the segment-start agent position and the agent moves at most
0.05∆t. Since d > 0.3∆t, triangle inequality gives dnext ≤d −(0.3 −0.05)∆t. From d0 = 0.25 m, contact must occur
by 0.8 s without a burst, regardless of heading commands. A lightweight geometry property test checks this bound
over arbitrary commands. It is not an additional neural experiment or a newly fitted warning metric.
This diagnostic matrix was registered after the original core launch, before complete core analysis. It is not part of
the original confirmatory registration and contributes no primary significance test. Eight new seeds 1200–1207 cross
the two core bearings with normal proportional/relay, turn-disabled and steering-and-burst-disabled interfaces on
healthy networks. The turn-disabled interface retains neural burst triggering; the other removes both neural actions.
Common uniforms, calibration and physical settings remain fixed. Every normal failure and every ablated success is
retained. The pair of ablations does not separately isolate burst necessity; the kinematic bound above establishes its
necessity for this plant. These checks do not establish connectome-specific superiority or biological escape.

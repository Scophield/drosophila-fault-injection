## Visual argument

1. A matches RF, seed, bearing and controller across healthy/25%/50% deletion. The signal is the recorded eight-cell, five-frame command mean, drawn as a post-step curve. Neural trigger, exact actuator onset and burst end have distinct markers.
2. B matches a verified P/R pair, including mask, uniforms, calibration, input mapping and trigger schedule. The panels compare complete feedback configurations, with the same world coordinates and simulation clock.
3. C reuses B's P trajectory at two endpoints. It pauses at 900 ms, continues to the exact first contact, then shows the latched outcome at 1000 ms. Contact-free at an endpoint is not permanent escape.
4. The overview edits these three clips; it introduces no additional experiment.

The archetype is a synchronized quantitative animation with motion as the main evidence and signal/timeline strips as supporting evidence. Selection is retrospective and illustrative. Cohort statistics are shown separately, with seed-cluster units.

## Geometry and timing

Python is the established project backend. Existing task motion and independent plant/input replay validate the exported original subsegments. Positions are interpolated only within those exact straight segments; heading remains at its segment-start value until the original update boundary. No new dynamics, random input or neural spikes are synthesized.

The physical contact criterion is 50 mm center separation. A radius-50-mm ring about the agent marks that boundary; decorative static fly/threat icons do not set physical scale. Agent/trail is teal, pursuer/trail coral, readout violet, and burst amber throughout. Text distinguishes all conditions and controllers.

An affected scene and its signal trace stop at first contact. The overall simulation clock can continue to H while the contact pose and result stay latched. Pauses and playback factors are explicitly separate from simulation time.

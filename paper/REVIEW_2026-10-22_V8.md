# Independent review of assessment-v8

**Recommendation: 4/10, reject in its current submission form; substantial revision warranted. Confidence: 4/5.**

**Assumed venue: IEEE ISCAS 2027, regular paper, Neural Networks and Neuromorphic Engineering.** The README explicitly names ISCAS 2027; IEEEtran conference mode, the five-page package and deadline context agree. This is an author-requested presubmission assessment, not an official IEEE review.

The revision resolves the missing controller-by-fault comparison, and its paired arithmetic reproduces. However, page five violates the submission rule, actuator timing/endpoint metadata need correction, and scientific significance remains limited by a fragile controller and an imperfect archive.

## Phase 1 - Understanding the paper

**Scope inspected.** I read `main.tex`, all six included section files, `refs.bib`, IEEEtran class/style headers and configuration, the v8 response, and the 14-page supplement. All main pages and supplementary figures/tables were inspected. No separate cover letter or appendix was found. Raw-trajectory analyses were rerun; connectome simulations and clean-environment installation were not.

1. **Problem.** Determine how assessment cohorts, observation exposure and closed-loop control laws alter the interpretation of software faults in a fixed fly-connectome spiking model.

2. **Claimed contributions.** Introduction, `intro.tex:14`, states:

   > "The contribution is empirical: we quantify how assessment choices change the interpretation of fault effects in this model."

   - "Archive reanalysis holds observations fixed": my reading is a descriptive sensitivity audit, not a newly identified fault-tolerance mechanism.
   - "Prospective tests cross two frozen control laws": the main new experiment directly compares healthy-to-fault losses and gains under each law.
   - "Paired task loss separates fault effects from baseline controller success": useful bookkeeping, although the interaction still depends mathematically on baseline success and floor effects.

3. **Method.** A Brian2 LIF model contains 138,639 neurons and 15,091,983 weighted connection rows. Prescribed open-loop drive stimulates 314 LC4/LPLC2 cells; eight descending neurons provide the readout. Five perturbation families change weights, delete rows or silence outputs. A separate synthetic pursuit environment converts neural activity into turning and a burst, with frozen calibration. Analyses distinguish transmission, activity excursions and collision-free survival, and separate unequal stopping times from observation-window truncation.

4. **Experimental setup.** The archived main sweep has 19 conditions including healthy, 12 trials each, at 600 ms; the scored fault/control mixture contains 288 records. Fresh experiments include nine passive calibration episodes, 48 first-campaign tests, 48 healthy controller controls, 12 refractory-variant runs, and 48 new faulty episodes combined with 24 reused healthy episodes. The factorial study has **six seed clusters**, two fixed bearings, two laws and three conditions; masks are shared across laws/bearings and nested across doses. Calibration uses seeds 0-2 and -60/-30/0 degrees; factorial testing uses seeds 12-17 and +/-30 degrees. Constants include 0.1-ms neural integration, 20-ms feedback, 1-s horizon, 0.05-m collision distance, 18.125-Hz trigger and 400-deg/s steering limit. Controls include readout disabling, offset-only/no-steering laws, a privileged geometric rule and a nonfunctional target-permuted graph. Conditional percentile bootstraps use 20,000 draws; a post-hoc sign-flip diagnostic enumerates 64 patterns. Compute uses a Xeon W-2223 CPU, NumPy/Brian2; new episode wall times sum to 6,162.4 s, while the successful resumed invocation took 1,727.1 s with three workers, excluding the interrupted invocation.

6. **Stated limitations.** One connectome; assumed receptive fields; asymmetric calibration; six factorial clusters; directional floor effects; shared noise/mask seeds; outcome-dependent common duration; historical calibration reuse, missing hub identities and undocumented time step; operational activity thresholds; incomplete public availability; bounded survival rather than eventual escape. These disclosures are substantive improvements.

## Phase 4 - Detailed findings

### Blockers and major acceptance risks

**B1. The main PDF violates the references-only fifth-page rule.** Page five contains Fig. 3, geometric-rule results and all of Sec. V. The official instructions explicitly reserve that page for references. This creates an administrative rejection risk independent of scientific quality. Compress methods and historical narrative, reduce the large title/overview footprint, and end all technical material on page four without reducing mandated typography. `tools/validate_revision.py:148` checks page count but does not enforce its computed `reference_only` flag; repair that validation condition. The previous successful preflight does not establish submission compliance.

**B2. The actuator description does not state the realized discrete-time protocol.** `methods.tex:43` says:

> "Burst speed is 0.35 m/s for 450 ms with fixed heading"

Yet `sim_vision/run_closedloop.py:138` evaluates bursts on 20-ms frame starts. Observed delays are 260 ms; four bursts with observed ends last 440 ms. Original/F2_25/-30/seed 17 triggers at 260 ms and starts bursting at 520 ms. Distinguish nominal scheduling from realized motion. Exact 250/450-ms actuation would require sub-frame handling and affected reruns.

At line 165, `burst_active_at_horizon` copies the final frame's flag. Six surviving factorial records are marked active although their burst ends exactly at 1000 ms. Supplement Sec. 7's healthy Original/gated counts are therefore **2/2 at the exact horizon, versus 4/3 during the final frame**. Correct derived reporting or relabel the interval; preserve raw records. Survival counts are unaffected. Test finer feedback steps before asserting timing-insensitive outcomes.

**B3. Independent confirmation is the main scientific acceptance risk.** Table I resolves the factorial gap, but +30 degrees and 50% deletion remain at floor; the 25% interaction has three nonzero clusters. This supports a conditional counterexample, with limited evidence beyond this operating point. Freeze a validation protocol, test fresh clusters and another bearing on each side, and report separation/trigger timing. Choose sample size for stated precision or meaningful effect. An independent receptive-field-map seed would test an important assumption.

**B4. Novelty must survive comparison with close connectome studies.** Sec. II currently says what the study separates, without demonstrating what prior analyses leave unanswered. [An's 2026 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.19.752860v1.full), DOI 10.64898/2026.09.19.752860, already varies task, network state and sparsification operator. [Jo's preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19153), arXiv:2610.19153, compares circuit and virtual-arena readouts and discusses damage matching. Neither reference is missing. Add a comparison of substrate, damage unit, exposure handling, controller intervention and inferential scope; reserve novelty for the actual same-record exposure analysis and paired controller-by-fault experiment. If pivotal archive effects remain headline empirical evidence, prospectively reproduce a small manifest-complete contrast or explicitly present them as archival audit findings.

### Equations and statistical audit

- **Eq. (1):** units and signs are consistent. For inter-event, non-refractory evolution, `g(t)=g(0) exp(-t/5 ms)` and the drive contribution to voltage is `g(0)/3 [exp(-t/20 ms)-exp(-t/5 ms)]`. State the refractory pause and reset because the continuous equations alone do not define the simulator.
- **Perturbations:** `E[exp(sigma Z-sigma^2/2)]=1`; independent row deletion has expected lost absolute contact mass fraction `f`. Neither statement implies an individual realization loses exactly that fraction.
- **Eq. (2):** joint passing is necessarily nested in transmission. Extra rejections quantify a scoring disagreement, not predictive improvement.
- **Eq. (3):** definitions are correct. I reproduced 0.381701 and 0.729443. Common duration remains outcome-dependent; it is not an unbiased fixed-horizon resource estimate.
- **Eq. (4):** the examples and zero-readout value -114.286 deg/s are correct. Specify when neural measurements, heading updates and motion occur within a frame.
- **Interaction:** at 25%, `(6-3)/12-(4-4)/12=0.25`. Exact bootstrap enumeration reproduces [0.0833,0.4167]; sign flipping gives p=0.25. These answer different inferential constructions, so their disagreement is not an arithmetic error. Neither establishes a population effect with six clusters. At 50%, the contrast reduces to the healthy-baseline difference.

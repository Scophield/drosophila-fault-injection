\section{Scope, Artifacts and Conclusion}
The evidence concerns one graph, an assumed RF family, a synthetic plant, unequal healthy reserve and sparse failures. Held-out steering RMSE of roughly $66^\circ$ limits bearing reconstruction. Walk-only contact by 0.8\,s reflects plant speeds. With one burst followed by slower walking, no contact through 1\,s does not establish sustained avoidance. Functional row loss establishes no biological tolerance, device upset rate, hardware safety, energy or area benefit; record consistency does not authenticate neural execution.

The generating snapshot\cite{artifactV10} preserves original sources, 576 neural records, 96 tank examples and frozen configurations. The fixed assessment revision\cite{artifactV10Horizon} provides schema guards, horizon-analysis sources and file manifests. Only fresh neural runs need external, hash-matched matrices. Third-party tables with unresolved redistribution terms are excluded. Generation metadata remain unchanged.

Connection loss shifted mean action triggers later, while identical trigger/burst schedules still yielded law-discordant contacts. Trigger delay alone did not explain collision; healthy reserve, finite follow-up and recalibration constrain interpretation. Frozen scalar warnings retained task-label errors. The checked interface makes supporting pairs and clocks testable across two plant schemas. This task-level assessment provides a basis for evaluating how future device-level nonidealities propagate through neuromorphic controllers to closed-loop behavior.

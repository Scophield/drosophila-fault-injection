\section{Introduction and Positioning}
How does matched connection loss in a connectome-derived spiking neural network (SNN) affect first contact as feedback, observation horizon and calibrated sensing change? We pair healthy and damaged runs, resolving neural commands, action timing and contact separately.

Shiu et al.\cite{shiu2024drosophila} establish connectome-derived LIF sensorimotor predictions; here outputs drive a synthetic pursuit--escape plant. An\cite{an2026sparse} studies task/state-dependent preservation under sparsification, and Jo\cite{jo2026simulating} couples damaged circuits to virtual-arena behavior. We measure paired geometric first contact under two frozen laws and a declared horizon, and predict subsequent contact from a 400-ms prefix. Task dependence and neural/behavioral dissociation themselves are not new claims.

DriveFI searches closed-loop safety violations\cite{jha2019drivefi}; SpikeFI, SpikingJET and enpheeph provide SNN fault injection\cite{spyrou2024spikefi,gogebakan2024spikingjet,colucci2022enpheeph}. Common random numbers\cite{glasserman1992crn}, trajectory monitoring\cite{donze2010breach}, benchmarking\cite{yik2023neurobench,dangelo2026embodied} and physical fault propagation\cite{chessa2026crosslayer} are established. Our interface complements these methods with executable pairing, timing and native-replay obligations.

We contribute: (i) stage-resolved paired timing/contact measurements, including law-discordant outcomes under shared schedules and horizon-sensitive, uncertain RF transfer; (ii) frozen-prefix warnings with 11/13 missed RF-0 contacts and cross-configuration false alarms; and (iii) executable pairing, timing and exposure contracts, tested on archived actuator/endpoint errors and a delayed tank. Descriptive timing shifts remain distinct from causal explanations; contracts test realized actuation against declared schedules.

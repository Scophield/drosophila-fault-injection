Table~\ref{tab:principles} distills five transferable principles. P1 and P2 follow from the flat F1 and F3 medians: computation lives in distributed convergence, not precise magnitudes, favoring dense low-precision, partially defective fabrics---consistent with hardware observations\cite{arfa2025hardwareaware,thieu2022effects}. P3 shifts the failure budget: disconnection costs are mostly latency here, so real-time loops should specify worst-case response time under edge loss, which Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}a reports directly. P4 is the most actionable: the only \emph{amplifying} failure mode comes from removing the highest-fanout units, so designs should cap high-degree influence and monitor output-envelope violation rather than liveness---over-activity while all units read ``active.'' P5 locates the single point of failure at the interface: each sensory channel alone suffices (68--79\%); front-end duplication buys more safety than any internal redundancy.

One methodological lesson: identical relative pathway decay in both graphs, yet only the biological one transmitting a decodable command, argues against certifying embodied controllers by topology alone; fault-injection campaigns belong in pre-deployment stages\cite{yik2023neurobench,medvedeva2025formalizing}, and the seed-exact sweep re-runs in minutes on other hardware mappings\cite{wang2025neuromorphic}.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
Our measurements inherit the LIF abstraction---single-compartment delta synapses, no plasticity, Poisson drive---and a single drive rate; doses kept the background regime intact, the hub analysis is descriptive, and the taxonomy excludes correlated damage and recovery. An evolved 138k-neuron controller keeps its median command rate under weight noise and unit death and three quarters of it at half its connections gone---given convergent wiring and one live sensory channel; its dangerous failure is hub-loss dysregulation: robustness lives in the wiring, not in precision parts.

\section{Candidate Design Implications for Embodied Neuromorphic Controllers}
\label{sec:implications}
Three of the five implications concern where redundancy pays. Because $50\%$ weight variability and $10\%$ unit-output silencing both leave the median command rate unchanged (99.4\% and 100.4\% of baseline), precision and per-unit yield can be traded against wiring redundancy: such a controller needs many converging routes, not accurate synapses, consistent with hardware observations\cite{arfa2025hardwareaware,thieu2022effects}. Disconnection is instead paid in latency rather than rate---$76.5\%$ of the rate survives at $50\%$ loss while latency rises $33\%$---so a real-time loop should budget the latency curve (Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}a).

The other two implications concern failure modes that per-unit monitoring misses. Hub silencing is the only \emph{amplifying} class: it passes transmission while doubling activity, so controllers should monitor the activity envelope rather than liveness and should regulate high-fanout units before deployment. Deafferentation is the opposite extreme: either channel alone retains 68--79\% of the median rate, but losing both drives the pathway to zero, making the sensor interface---not the synaptic fabric---the first redundancy candidate. Because relative pathway decay is identical while only the biological graph yields a decodable command, fault injection belongs pre-deployment\cite{yik2023neurobench,medvedeva2025formalizing}.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
Our LIF abstraction fixes one drive rate, the hub analysis is descriptive, and correlated damage and recovery are excluded. The instrumented pathway is one of many, so the tolerance ordering is a hypothesis for other pathways rather than a whole-brain law, and its documented vulnerability to aging\cite{gaitanidis2025drosophila} suggests tolerance is not a fixed property of the wiring either. Under the tested drive the median command rate survives weight noise and unit silencing and retains 76.5\% at 50\% loss; the dangerous failure is hub over-activity, and a synthetic loop shows preserved transmission need not preserve behavior.

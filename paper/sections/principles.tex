Table~\ref{tab:principles} distills five candidate design implications. P1 and P2 follow from the flat F1 and F3 medians: computation lives in distributed convergence, not precise magnitudes, consistent with hardware observations\cite{arfa2025hardwareaware,thieu2022effects}. P3: disconnection costs are mostly latency, so real-time loops should budget for the median latency growth that Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}a reports. P4: removing the highest-fanout units is the only \emph{amplifying} failure mode, so cap high-degree influence and monitor output-envelope violation rather than liveness. P5: each sensory channel alone suffices (68--79\%); front-end duplication buys more margin than internal redundancy.

One methodological lesson: identical relative pathway decay, yet only the biological graph transmitting a decodable command, argues against certifying embodied controllers by topology alone; fault-injection campaigns belong in pre-deployment stages\cite{yik2023neurobench,medvedeva2025formalizing}.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
Our measurements inherit the LIF abstraction---single-compartment units, no plasticity, Poisson drive, one drive rate; doses kept the background regime intact, the hub analysis is descriptive, and correlated damage and recovery are excluded. Under the tested drive (sustained, well-above-threshold), the controller keeps its median command rate under weight noise, unit death, and 50\% connection loss; its dangerous failure is hub-loss dysregulation.

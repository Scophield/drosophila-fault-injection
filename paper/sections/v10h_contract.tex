\section{Executable Evidence Contract}
A campaign declares seed, condition, law, fault, horizon, frame duration and resource unit. Adapters bind native traces to these identities, sources, maps, calibration, input/mask digests, exposure, resources and first failure (Fig.~\ref{fig:v10f-system}(d)). Common checks test these fields and pairing; native replay independently regenerates inputs and plant transitions. Hashes bind bytes, not execution. Shared variates need not yield identical feedback-dependent events; warning prefixes exclude future labels.

Native replay checks 250-ms trigger-to-onset delay, 450-ms burst duration and interval-consistent horizon flags. In 72 separate historical records it finds 17 delays of 260\,ms, four completed bursts of 440\,ms and six incorrect horizon flags. These overlapping cases are not pooled with current cohorts: nominal parameters would misstate fast-motion duration or endpoint actuation. Common-schema findings retain row indices; native semantics remain adapter obligations.

The tank obeys $\dot h=I-u-0.04h$, $h(0)=0.5$, safe interval $[0.2,0.8]$ (\reuseRef(a)). Eight seeds $\times$ two inflows $\times$ two laws $\times$ three sensor-deletion levels give 96 examples; deleted samples hold their last value. Analytic replay checks accounting and 150-ms actuation on a 100-ms grid (\reuseRef(b)). All remain safe, demonstrating reuse rather than neural tolerance.

\reuseRef(c) reports eleven common-checker and three native-only cases per adapter, not general coverage. A synthetic zero-neural-readout logger passes input/plant replay without neural dynamics: trace consistency cannot authenticate execution, unlogged state or safety.

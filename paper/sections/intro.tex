\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{P}{hysical} intelligence---agents whose perception, decision, and action loops close in the physical world under tight energy and latency budgets---demands controllers that remain robust on imperfect hardware\cite{yuan2026embodiedr,guo2026neurovla}. Neuromorphic systems promise the required efficiency\cite{katti2026neuromorphic,gerven2025neuromorphic}, and spiking implementations on Loihi~2 and FPGA substrates now cover vision and control workloads\cite{wang2025neuromorphic,arfa2025hardwareaware,gautam2025neurocorex}. What remains poorly quantified is how \emph{wiring} determines robustness: engineered spiking networks are designed task-by-task, so their tolerance to weight noise, synapse loss, and unit death must be re-measured per design\cite{thieu2022effects,yunusoglu2025neuromorphic}. Complete, signed wiring diagrams of whole brains are now public, and point-neuron models built on them reproduce realistic dynamics without training\cite{lin2024network}; an evolved connectome is thus a fully specified, field-tested controller and a reference point for benchmarking the fault tolerance of engineered designs.

We perform, to our knowledge, the first systematic \emph{fault-injection} study on such a controller: the released leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) whole-brain model of \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model}}, which instantiates the complete FlyWire connectome\cite{lin2024network}. We instrument a behavioral task---the visuomotor escape pathway, in which looming-sensitive visual interneurons (LC4, LPLC2) converge onto descending command neurons (DNp01, DNa02, DNp04, DNb01) that trigger take-off\cite{shuang2023opplod,fu2020complementary}---and score outcome as command-population firing rate. The pathway is a complete sensor-to-command controller whose failure modes map directly onto hardware fault classes: weight imprecision, broken interconnect, dead cores, and dead sensors.

We inject five fault families (Section~III-C), each over 12 seeded 600\,ms trials, and separate wiring specificity from generic topology with two controls: an edge-permuted Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi (ER) null network with identical weight and out-degree statistics, and an analytic multi-hop pathway-mass computation. Our contributions: (i) a fault-injection framework mapping hardware failure modes onto a biologically exact 138k-neuron controller; (ii) the first behavioral-grade tolerance curves of a complete connectome (50\% weight noise absorbed, 50\% synapse loss retains 76\%, 10\% unit loss invisible); top-1\% hub loss instead produces a $2.5\times$ \emph{activity runaway}, and whole-channel deafferentation is the only catastrophic mode; (iii) a decomposition showing that linear graph redundancy explains neither specificity nor graceful degradation, implicating threshold amplification in convergent spiking dynamics; and (iv) five design principles for fault-tolerant neuromorphic controllers.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{P}{hysical} intelligence---agents whose perception, decision, and action loops close in the physical world under tight energy and latency budgets---demands controllers that remain robust on imperfect hardware\cite{yuan2026embodiedr,guo2026neurovla}. Neuromorphic systems promise the required efficiency\cite{gerven2025neuromorphic}, and spiking implementations on Loihi~2 and FPGA substrates now cover vision and control workloads\cite{wang2025neuromorphic,arfa2025hardwareaware,gautam2025neurocorex}. How \emph{wiring} determines robustness remains poorly quantified: engineered networks are designed task-by-task, so their fault tolerance must be re-measured per design\cite{thieu2022effects,yunusoglu2025neuromorphic}. Complete signed wiring diagrams of whole brains are public, and point-neuron models built on them reproduce realistic dynamics without training\cite{shiu2024drosophila,lin2024network}; an evolved connectome is thus a field-tested controller and a reference for engineered fault tolerance.

The released leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) whole-brain model of \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}\cite{shiu2024drosophila}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model}}, which instantiates the complete FlyWire connectome\cite{lin2024network}, has already been used for targeted computational silencings\cite{shiu2024drosophila} and for network-dismantling studies of a derived sugar-sensing subnetwork\cite{adler2026latent}; we sweep five \emph{hardware-motivated fault classes} on the fixed whole-brain model, scored jointly for transmission and activity-envelope compliance. We instrument the visuomotor escape pathway: looming-sensitive visual interneurons (LC4, LPLC2) converge onto descending command neurons (DNp01, DNa02, DNp04, DNb01) whose activation evokes take-off and escape steering\cite{simpson2023descending}. Its failure modes map onto hardware fault classes: weight imprecision, broken interconnect, dead cores, dead sensors.

We inject five fault families (Section~III-C), each over 12 seeded 600\,ms trials, against an edge-permuted null and an analytic pathway-mass control. Our contributions: (i) a fault-injection framework mapping hardware failure modes onto a connectome-constrained 138k-neuron LIF model; and (ii) tolerance curves of descending-neuron output in a complete connectome (50\% weight noise absorbed; 50\% connection loss retains 76.5\% of command rate; 10\% random-unit silencing leaves the median unchanged), while top-1\% hub silencing produces a $2.5\times$ \emph{over-activity} that passes transmission yet violates the activity envelope, and deafferentation fails both criteria. We further (iii) show that graph redundancy alone explains neither specificity nor graceful degradation (threshold amplification is a candidate account), (iv) distill five design implications, and (v) demonstrate in a synthetic closed loop that preserved transmission can still fail the task.

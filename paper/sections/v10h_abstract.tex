The public release of the complete adult fruit-fly brain connectome enables brain-scale spiking-network studies, motivating closed-loop assessment of connection loss. We test a connectome-derived controller in synthetic pursuit--escape and check record consistency. Across 576 test episodes in paired campaigns, the baseline mapping at 50\% connection-row deletion yields 11/48 proportional and 0/48 relay collisions by 1\,s, despite paired P/R trigger/burst schedules. The prespecified secondary relay-minus-proportional net-loss difference is $-22.9$ percentage points (95\% seed-cluster CI $[-33.3,-12.5]$). Two separately recalibrated mappings yield smaller, uncertain point estimates; unequal healthy clearance, recalibration and different test cohorts limit attribution. A frozen command-rate warning detects only 2/13 baseline collisions across all tested deletion levels. In a separate author-designed fly/delayed-tank catalogue, checks plus model-specific replay detect 81/87 corruptions versus 36/87 for trusted headers, accepting all 20 distinct valid inputs (six misses). These results quantify finite-horizon collisions and the gains and limits of record checks.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Baseline: The Connectome Is a Specific, Low-Latency Controller}
Driving LC4 and LPLC2 at 150\,Hz produces a command rate of $94.7$\,Hz (median over 12 trials) with 4.05\,ms first-spike latency, far above $\theta$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:baseline}). An equal-sized random set, driven identically, yields only $8.2$\,Hz on the same readout while producing \emph{more} total activity (293k vs.\ 40k spikes): the readout is pathway-specific, not generic arousal. When both sensory channels are silenced (F5\textsubscript{both}), the command rate collapses to zero in every trial; the residual 21.2k spikes are, by construction, the stimulated sensory cells themselves (the rest of the brain starts at zero baseline and receives no input), so the score measures transmission through the instrumented pathway.

\subsection{Tolerance Curves: Noise Is Absorbed, Disconnection Degrades Gracefully}
Figure~\ref{fig:curves} shows dose--response curves for the two most consequential families; F1 and F3 responses are flat in median (Table~\ref{tab:principles}). Weight variability (F1) leaves the median unchanged: at $\sigma{=}0.5$ the rate is $94.2$\,Hz (healthy: 94.7\,Hz), the trial dispersion widens (interdecile 90.4--100.8 vs.\ 94.0--95.6\,Hz), and total activity rises only from 39.7k to 41.6k spikes. Random connection deletion (F2) degrades gracefully: 5\% and 10\% loss leave the rate at 91.9 and 93.1\,Hz, 25\% loss retains $84.5$\,Hz (89.2\% of baseline), and even 50\% loss retains $72.4$\,Hz (76.5\%), with all 12 trials passing transmission in every condition (weakest 63.1 and 68.1\,Hz, above $\theta$ by $\geq$22\%). Disconnection's cost appears predominantly in latency: 4.05 to 5.4\,ms at 50\% loss (+33\%), consistent with weakened aggregate drive rather than longer residual routes alone; which dominates a task-level error budget is workload-dependent. Random unit silencing (F3) up to 10\%---nearly 14{,}000 neurons cut off from their targets---leaves the median unchanged (95.1 vs.\ 94.7\,Hz): command drive integrates over hundreds of converging populations, and no tested random slice is load-bearing. The wide 1{,}299-neuron descending layer tracks the same pattern (2{,}941 vs.\ 1{,}918 at 50\%), so the graded response is a property of command encoding, not the eight-neuron readout. Aggregation masks composition: four of eight command cells carry 96.6\% of healthy readout spikes; at 50\% loss the smallest collapse (158$\to$10, 26$\to$0) while silent cells are recruited (1$\to$58, 0$\to$21); the curves are a descending-population response, not behavior.

\subsection{Hub Silencing Produces Over-Activity, Not Silence}
The dose--response of hub silencing (F4) is non-monotonic and qualitatively different from every distributed fault (Figs.~\ref{fig:curves}b, \ref{fig:topology}b). Silencing the top 1\% of cells by outgoing contact count (1{,}386 neurons, 9.1\% of connections) \emph{increases} the command rate to $122.3$\,Hz (+29\%, interdecile 121.1--123.9\,Hz across trials) and more than doubles global activity (99.9k spikes, $2.52\times$ healthy; Mann--Whitney vs.\ healthy $p<10^{-4}$, as for F2$_{50}$ and F5$_{\mathrm{both}}$); at 2\% the rate stays elevated (117.1\,Hz, activity 42.4k); at 5\% (22.4\% of connections) both metrics return to near-baseline (94.2\,Hz, 36.5k). A matched-disconnection contrast suggests the effect is not disconnection alone: random 10\% unit silencing disconnects 10.0\% of connections (more than the hub set's 9.1\%) yet leaves the readout unchanged (95.1\,Hz); a control matching cell count and excitation/inhibition composition is still needed. The removed hub output is inhibitory-rich (62\% vs.\ 40\% network-wide), making de-inhibition the leading hypothesis for the over-drive. We therefore score \emph{transmission} (rate $\geq\theta$) and an \emph{activity envelope} (rate and total activity within $2\times$ of healthy) separately: hub silencing at 1\% passes transmission while violating the envelope in all 12 trials (persists for multipliers $1.5\times$--$2\times$); every distributed fault passes both; F5\textsubscript{both} fails both. The envelope violation is the dangerous mode: output exceeds its envelope while every per-unit monitor reads ``active.'' The same silencing in the permuted null produces no such signature, tying the over-activity to biological hub structure.

\subsection{What the Wiring Contributes: Null Network and Pathway Analysis}
The permuted null dissociates specificity from redundancy: out-degrees, weights, and the in-degree histogram survive, convergence is reassigned, and its median command rate is 0.0\,Hz in every tested condition, with no trial reaching $\theta$; its broad 1{,}299-neuron readout falls to background (45.5 spikes per trial vs.\ 2{,}941 healthy) and global activity to about 60\% of the biological level (23.8k vs.\ 39.7k)---little more than the 21.2k the driven cells produce with all outputs severed (F5\textsubscript{both}): the null receives the same drive, but only ${\sim}2.6$k spikes propagate, and the descending readout stays at background. Pathway mass quantifies the convergence advantage: 5.8$\times$ (1-hop) and 17.7$\times$ (4-hop) more route mass into the descending layer. Yet \emph{relative} pathway-mass decay is nearly identical (at $f{=}0.5$, $k{=}8$: 0.3\% vs.\ 0.4\% of baseline; Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}a), while output degrades far more slowly (76\% at $f{=}0.5$ against 5.8\% four-hop route mass)---ruling out graph redundancy alone as the mechanism. A candidate account is threshold amplification: with $N$ converging afferents and per-synapse survival $p$, aggregate drive scales as $Np$ while $k$-hop route mass decays as $p^k$ in expectation (6.25\% at $k{=}4$, $p{=}0.5$), and the saturating threshold compresses drive changes into small rate changes. Retention above proportional scaling (76.5\% at $p{=}0.5$, 89.2\% at $p{=}0.75$) is consistent with this account but does not establish it: in an excitatory--inhibitory network even linear signal flow need not scale with $p$, since deleting inhibitory connections offsets excitatory loss. Disentangling these requires E/I-balanced deletion and drive-strength sweeps.

\subsection{From Transmission to Behavior: A Closed-Loop Escape Demo}
Transmission retention need not imply task capability, so we close the loop synthetically: per-cell looming rates over the 314 instrumented LC4/LPLC2 cells (assumed receptive fields), the full network in the loop, and a frozen (two training azimuths) left--right difference across the eight command cells steering a planar agent away from a pursuing threat, with a ballistic takeoff triggered by the 8-cell \emph{average} rate against a scene-calibrated threshold ($\theta_{\mathrm{demo}}{=}25.6$\,Hz, the training trajectories' median early response; open-loop $\theta{=}51.4$\,Hz was calibrated for full-pool drive and does not transfer). It escapes 24/24 at four azimuths (six seeds; median miss 0.09--0.11\,m vs.\ 0.05\,m contact; Fig.~\ref{fig:demo}), 12/12 at held-out azimuths, 0/3 frontal (blind spot). Every control collapses (0/12: steering, both branches, reversed sides); a scene-reading hand rule serves as a reference baseline (12/12). Faults transfer with a threshold cliff: 25\% loss gives 10/12 and 50\% gives 0/12---the command rate stays above the open-loop threshold yet falls below $\theta_{\mathrm{demo}}$, so preserved transmission does not imply preserved behavior; hub silencing preserves escape (12/12) with activity rising to $1.7\times$ healthy, inside the $2\times$ envelope. This is a preliminary synthetic demonstration, not a behavioral validation (assumed encoding, unmodeled gaze, scripted envelope, kinematic agent).

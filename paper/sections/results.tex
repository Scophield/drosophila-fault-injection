\section{Results}
\subsection{Baseline: The Connectome Is a Specific, Low-Latency Controller}
Driving LC4 and LPLC2 at 150\,Hz produces a command rate of $94.7$\,Hz (median over 12 trials) with 4.05\,ms first-spike latency, far above $\theta$. An equal-sized random set, driven identically, yields only $8.2$\,Hz on the same readout while producing \emph{more} total activity (293k vs.\ 40k spikes), so the readout is pathway-specific rather than a generic arousal response. With both channels silenced (F5\textsubscript{both}) it collapses to zero in every trial, and the residual 21.2k spikes are the stimulated sensory cells themselves, so the score measures transmission through the pathway.

\subsection{Tolerance Curves: Noise Is Absorbed, Disconnection Degrades Gracefully}
Figure~\ref{fig:curves} shows dose--response curves for the two most consequential families; F1 and F3 are flat (Section~\ref{sec:implications}). At $\sigma{=}0.5$ weight variability leaves the median unchanged ($94.2$ vs.\ 94.7\,Hz) but widens trial dispersion (interdecile 90.4--100.8 vs.\ 94.0--95.6\,Hz) and raises total activity only from 39.7k to 41.6k spikes. Random connection deletion degrades gracefully: 5\% and 10\% loss leave 91.9 and 93.1\,Hz, 25\% retains $84.5$\,Hz (89.2\%), and 50\% retains $72.4$\,Hz (76.5\%), with all 12 trials passing transmission in every condition (weakest 63.1 and 68.1\,Hz, $\geq$22\% above $\theta$). The cost appears predominantly in latency---4.05 to 5.4\,ms at 50\% loss (+33\%)---consistent with weakened aggregate drive rather than longer residual routes; Silencing 10\% of units at random---nearly 14{,}000 neurons cut off from their targets---also leaves the median unchanged (95.1\,Hz): command drive integrates over hundreds of converging populations, and no tested random slice is load-bearing. The 1{,}299-neuron descending layer tracks the same pattern (2{,}941 vs.\ 1{,}918 spikes at 50\%), so the graded response belongs to command encoding, not to the eight-neuron readout.

\subsection{Hub Silencing Is Resolved by Sign, Not by Size}
The dose--response of hub silencing (F4) is the only non-monotonic family (Figs.~\ref{fig:curves}b, \ref{fig:topology}b). Silencing the top 1\% of cells by outgoing contact count (1{,}386 neurons, 9.1\% of connections, 62\% of them inhibitory) raises the command rate to $119.1$\,Hz (+26\%) and global activity to 49.3k spikes ($1.25\times$ healthy; Mann--Whitney $p<10^{-4}$); at 2\% (13.2\% of connections) it stays elevated (114.8\,Hz, 38.9k) and at 5\% returns to baseline (94.0\,Hz, 34.1k). Splitting by sign explains both: silencing only the 765 inhibitory hubs (5.6\% of connections) drives the readout to $168.2$\,Hz and activity to 846.9k spikes ($21.4\times$) in every trial, whereas silencing only the 621 excitatory hubs (3.5\%) lowers them to $88.6$\,Hz and 32.3k ($0.82\times$). The full set thus nearly cancels a runaway against a depression, which is why aggregate removal looks mild while its inhibitory half is extreme. Identity, not mass, drives this: 765 random inhibitory cells remove 0.8\% of connections and change nothing (95.2\,Hz), and even 8{,}889 random inhibitory cells removing 8.6\%---more than the hub set---give only a $1.27\times$ median excursion (102.4\,Hz, 50.3k), though one of twelve trials runs to 327k. A set matched to the hubs in size and E/I composition (94.5\,Hz, 40.1k, 1.1\% of connections) and random 10\% unit silencing (95.1\,Hz) likewise do nothing. Scoring \emph{transmission} (rate $\geq\theta$) and an \emph{activity envelope} (within $2\times$ healthy) separately, the inhibitory-hub condition alone passes the first and violates the second in all 12 trials; every distributed fault passes both, and F5\textsubscript{both} fails both.

\subsection{What the Wiring Contributes: Null Network and Pathway Analysis}
The permuted null dissociates specificity from redundancy: out-degrees, weights and the in-degree histogram survive but convergence is reassigned, and no trial reaches $\theta$ (median 0.0\,Hz). Its readout falls to background (45.5 vs.\ 2{,}941 spikes per trial) and global activity to 60\% (23.8k vs.\ 39.7k), barely above the 21.2k the driven cells produce with all outputs severed. Pathway mass quantifies the convergence advantage: 5.8$\times$ (1-hop) and 17.7$\times$ (4-hop) more route mass into the descending layer. Yet \emph{relative} pathway-mass decay is nearly identical (at $f{=}0.5$, $k{=}8$: 0.3\% vs.\ 0.4\% of baseline; Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}a), while output degrades far more slowly (76.5\% at $f{=}0.5$ against 5.8\% four-hop route mass), ruling out graph redundancy alone. A candidate account is compressive recruitment: with $N$ converging afferents and survival $p$, aggregate drive scales as $Np$ while $k$-hop route mass decays as $p^k$ (6.25\% at $k{=}4$, $p{=}0.5$), and a threshold followed by saturation compresses drive changes into sub-proportional rate changes. Retention exceeds proportional scaling at every dose and increasingly so as damage weakens ($1.5\times$ proportional at $p{=}0.5$, $3.6\times$ at $p{=}0.25$, $19\times$ at $p{=}0.05$): mild deletion is buffered almost entirely, severe deletion bites. At $p{=}0.5$ the buffering is drive-invariant---raising drive from 75 to 300\,Hz lifts the healthy rate from 75.2 to 113.0\,Hz but leaves retention at 73.8\%, 77.6\%, 76.5\%---so it is set by convergence geometry rather than by operating point, although a common saturated regime at all three drives cannot be excluded. A single power law fits only the severe end ($\alpha\approx0.4$), so we report compression as dose-dependent rather than as a global exponent.

\subsection{From Transmission to Behavior: A Closed-Loop Escape Demo}
Transmission retention need not imply task capability, so we close the loop: looming rates over the 314 LC4/LPLC2 cells (assumed receptive fields) drive the full network, a frozen left--right difference across the eight command cells steers a planar agent away from a pursuing threat, and takeoff fires when their mean rate crosses $\theta_{\mathrm{demo}}{=}25.6$\,Hz (median peak of six training episodes; the open-loop $\theta{=}51.4$\,Hz does not transfer). It escapes 24/24 across four azimuths ($\pm30^\circ$, $\pm60^\circ$; six seeds; median miss 0.09--0.11\,m against a 0.05\,m radius; Fig.~\ref{fig:demo}), including 12/12 at the untrained azimuths ($+30^\circ$, $+60^\circ$), and 0/3 for a frontal approach (blind spot). Every control collapses (0/12 each), while a scene-reading hand rule reaches 12/12. Faults transfer as a threshold cliff rather than a graded rate loss: 25\% loss escapes 10/12 and 50\% escapes 0/12, although the same 50\% loss retains $72.4$\,Hz open-loop. Under single-target drive the F2$_{50}$ readout peaks at ${\approx}38$\,Hz and first reaches $\theta_{\mathrm{demo}}$ only at 420--500\,ms, when the threat is 7--11\,cm away---too late for the 250\,ms aiming delay and 450\,ms takeoff---so preserved transmission does not imply preserved behavior and the open-loop criterion is the wrong certificate for a closed loop. Hub silencing, by contrast, preserves escape (12/12) at $1.7\times$ the loop's healthy activity ($1.25\times$ open-loop), inside the $2\times$ envelope. Its inhibitory half is the dangerous mode: 6/12 escapes, 1.24M spikes ($77\times$), takeoff firing in every trial while the median heading change falls from 97$^\circ$ to 64$^\circ$, and all six failures at the two trained azimuths---the runaway does not silence the controller, it destroys the steering signal. This is a preliminary demonstration, not a behavioral validation (assumed encoding, scripted envelope, kinematic agent).

\section{Results}
\subsection{Baseline: The Connectome Is a Specific, Low-Latency Controller}
Driving LC4 and LPLC2 at 150\,Hz produces a command rate of $94.7$\,Hz (median over 12 trials; interdecile 94.0--95.6\,Hz) with 4.05\,ms median first-spike latency, far above $\theta$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:baseline}). An equal-sized random set, driven identically, yields only $8.2$\,Hz on the same readout while producing \emph{more} total activity (293k vs.\ 40k spikes): the readout is pathway-specific, not generic arousal. When both sensory channels are silenced (F5\textsubscript{both}), the command rate collapses to zero in every trial; the residual 21.2k spikes are, by construction, the stimulated sensory cells themselves---the rest of the brain, initialized at zero baseline, receives no input---so the score measures transmission through the instrumented pathway, not global silencing.

\subsection{Tolerance Curves: Noise Is Absorbed, Disconnection Degrades Gracefully}
Figure~\ref{fig:curves} shows dose--response curves for the two most consequential families; F1 and F3 responses are flat in median (Table~\ref{tab:principles}). Weight variability (F1) leaves the median unchanged: at $\sigma{=}0.5$ the rate is $94.2$\,Hz (healthy: 94.7\,Hz), the trial dispersion widens (interdecile 90.4--100.8 vs.\ 94.0--95.6\,Hz), and total activity rises only from 39.7k to 41.6k spikes. Random connection deletion (F2) degrades gracefully: 5\% and 10\% loss leave the rate at 91.9 and 93.1\,Hz, 25\% loss retains $84.5$\,Hz (89.2\% of baseline), and even 50\% loss retains $72.4$\,Hz (76.5\%), with all 12 trials passing transmission in every condition (weakest trials 63.1 and 68.1\,Hz---above $\theta$ by $\geq$22\%). Disconnection's cost appears predominantly in latency: 4.05 to 5.4\,ms at 50\% loss (+33\%), consistent with recruitment of longer residual multi-hop routes; which dominates a task-level error budget is workload-dependent. Random unit loss (F3) up to 10\%---nearly 14{,}000 neurons with all their synapses---leaves the median unchanged (95.1 vs.\ 94.7\,Hz): command drive integrates over hundreds of converging populations, and no tested random slice is load-bearing. The wide 1{,}299-neuron descending layer tracks the same pattern (2{,}941 spikes healthy vs.\ 1{,}918 at 50\% loss), so the graded response is a property of command encoding, not of the eight-neuron readout. Aggregation also masks composition: four of the eight command cells carry 96.6\% of healthy readout spikes, and at 50\% connection loss the smallest contributors collapse (158$\to$10, 26$\to$0) while silent cells are recruited (1$\to$58, 0$\to$21); the curves are thus a descending-population response, not behavior.

\subsection{Hub Loss Produces Runaway, Not Silence}
The dose--response of hub removal (F4) is non-monotonic and qualitatively different from every distributed fault (Figs.~\ref{fig:curves}b, \ref{fig:topology}b). Removing the top 1\% of cells by outgoing synapse count (1{,}386 neurons, 9.1\% of connections) \emph{increases} the command rate to $122.3$\,Hz (+29\%, interdecile 121.1--123.9\,Hz across trials) and more than doubles global activity (99.9k spikes, $2.52\times$ healthy; Mann--Whitney vs.\ healthy $p<10^{-4}$, as for F2$_{50}$ and F5$_{\mathrm{both}}$); at 2\% the rate stays elevated (117.1\,Hz, activity 42.4k); at 5\% (22.4\% of connections) both metrics return to near-baseline (94.2\,Hz, 36.5k). A matched-scale contrast rules out a size effect: random 10\% unit ablation disconnects 10.0\% of connections (more than the hub set's 9.1\%) yet leaves the readout unchanged (95.1\,Hz). Hub lesions do not starve the pathway of drive---they remove broadly connected inhibitory regulation, and spared circuitry over-drives the command layer: 62\% of removed hub output connections are inhibitory (40\% network-wide). We therefore score \emph{transmission} (rate $\geq\theta$) and an \emph{activity envelope} (rate and total activity within $2\times$ of healthy) separately: hub loss at 1\% passes transmission while violating the envelope in all 12 trials (persisting for any multiplier between $1.5\times$ and $2\times$); every distributed fault passes both; F5\textsubscript{both} fails both. The envelope violation is the engineered system's dangerous mode: output exceeds its envelope while every per-unit monitor reads ``active.'' The same lesion in the permuted null produces no such signature, tying the runaway to biological hub structure, not degree heterogeneity.

\subsection{What the Wiring Contributes: Null Network and Pathway Analysis}
The permuted null dissociates specificity from redundancy: out-degrees, weights, and the in-degree histogram survive, convergence is reassigned, and it transmits \emph{nothing} to the command layer (median 0.0\,Hz in every condition; no null trial reaches $\theta$); its broad 1{,}299-neuron readout falls to background (45.5 spikes per trial vs.\ 2{,}941 healthy) and global activity to about 60\% of the biological level (23.8k vs.\ 39.7k)---little more than the 21.2k the driven cells produce with all outputs severed (F5\textsubscript{both}): the permuted network receives the same drive, but only ${\sim}2.6$k spikes propagate and none converge onto descending output. Pathway mass quantifies the convergence advantage: 5.8$\times$ (1-hop) and 17.7$\times$ (4-hop) more route mass into the descending layer than the null. Yet \emph{relative} pathway-mass decay is nearly identical in both graphs (at $f{=}0.5$, $k{=}8$: 0.3\% vs.\ 0.4\% of baseline; Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}a), while output degrades far more slowly than pathway mass (76\% at $f{=}0.5$ against 5.8\% four-hop route mass)---ruling out graph redundancy alone as the mechanism. A candidate account is threshold amplification: with $N$ converging afferents and per-synapse survival $p$, aggregate drive scales as $Np$ while $k$-hop route mass decays as $p^k$ in expectation (6.25\% at $k{=}4$, $p{=}0.5$), and the saturating threshold compresses drive changes into small rate changes. Retention above proportional scaling (76.5\% at $p{=}0.5$, 89.2\% at $p{=}0.75$) is consistent with this account but does not establish it: in an excitatory--inhibitory network even linear signal flow need not scale with $p$, since deleting inhibitory connections offsets excitatory loss. Disentangling disinhibition, direct sensory$\to$DN edges, and threshold amplification requires E/I-balanced deletion and drive-strength sweeps.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Baseline: The Connectome Is a Specific, Low-Latency Controller}
Driving LC4 and LPLC2 at 150\,Hz produces a command rate of $94.7$\,Hz (median over 12 trials) with 4.05\,ms first-spike latency, far above $\theta$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:baseline}). An equal-sized random set, driven identically, yields only $8.2$\,Hz on the same readout while producing \emph{more} total activity (293k vs.\ 40k spikes): the readout is pathway-specific, not generic arousal. With both channels silenced (F5\textsubscript{both}) it collapses to zero in every trial, and the residual 21.2k spikes are the stimulated sensory cells themselves, so the score measures transmission through the pathway.

\subsection{Tolerance Curves: Noise Is Absorbed, Disconnection Degrades Gracefully}
Figure~\ref{fig:curves} shows dose--response curves for the two most consequential families; F1 and F3 are flat in median (Section~\ref{sec:implications}). At $\sigma{=}0.5$ weight variability leaves the median unchanged ($94.2$ vs.\ 94.7\,Hz) but widens trial dispersion (interdecile 90.4--100.8 vs.\ 94.0--95.6\,Hz) and raises total activity only from 39.7k to 41.6k spikes. Random connection deletion degrades gracefully: 5\% and 10\% loss leave 91.9 and 93.1\,Hz, 25\% retains $84.5$\,Hz (89.2\%), and 50\% retains $72.4$\,Hz (76.5\%), with all 12 trials passing transmission in every condition (weakest 63.1 and 68.1\,Hz, $\geq$22\% above $\theta$). The cost appears predominantly in latency---4.05 to 5.4\,ms at 50\% loss (+33\%)---consistent with weakened aggregate drive rather than longer residual routes; which dominates a task-level error budget is workload-dependent. Silencing 10\% of units at random---nearly 14{,}000 neurons cut off from their targets---also leaves the median unchanged (95.1\,Hz): command drive integrates over hundreds of converging populations, and no tested random slice is load-bearing. The 1{,}299-neuron descending layer tracks the same pattern (2{,}941 vs.\ 1{,}918 spikes at 50\%), so the graded response belongs to command encoding, not to the eight-neuron readout.

\subsection{Hub Silencing Produces Over-Activity, Not Silence}
The dose--response of hub silencing (F4) is non-monotonic and qualitatively different from every distributed fault (Figs.~\ref{fig:curves}b, \ref{fig:topology}b). Silencing the top 1\% of cells by outgoing contact count (1{,}386 neurons, 9.1\% of connections) \emph{increases} the command rate to $122.3$\,Hz (+29\%, interdecile 121.1--123.9\,Hz across trials) and more than doubles global activity (99.9k spikes, $2.52\times$ healthy; Mann--Whitney $p<10^{-4}$, as for F2$_{50}$ and F5$_{\mathrm{both}}$); at 2\% the rate stays elevated (117.1\,Hz, 42.4k); at 5\% (22.4\% of connections) both metrics return to near-baseline (94.2\,Hz, 36.5k). The effect is not disconnection alone: random 10\% unit silencing disconnects 10.0\% of connections (more than the hub set's 9.1\%) yet leaves the readout unchanged (95.1\,Hz), although a control matching cell count and E/I composition is still needed. Because the removed output is inhibitory-rich (62\% vs.\ 40\% network-wide), de-inhibition is the leading hypothesis. We therefore score \emph{transmission} (rate $\geq\theta$) and an \emph{activity envelope} (rate and total activity within $2\times$ healthy) separately: 1\% hub silencing passes transmission while violating the envelope in all 12 trials (persists for multipliers $1.5\times$--$2\times$); every distributed fault passes both; F5\textsubscript{both} fails both. The envelope violation is the dangerous mode: output exceeds its envelope while every per-unit monitor reads ``active,'' and the null shows no such signature.

\subsection{What the Wiring Contributes: Null Network and Pathway Analysis}
The permuted null dissociates specificity from redundancy: out-degrees, weights, and the in-degree histogram survive, convergence is reassigned, and its median command rate is 0.0\,Hz in every tested condition, with no trial reaching $\theta$. Its 1{,}299-neuron readout falls to background (45.5 spikes per trial vs.\ 2{,}941 healthy) and global activity to 60\% of the biological level (23.8k vs.\ 39.7k)---barely above the 21.2k the driven cells produce with all outputs severed, so only ${\sim}2.6$k spikes propagate. Pathway mass quantifies the convergence advantage: 5.8$\times$ (1-hop) and 17.7$\times$ (4-hop) more route mass into the descending layer. Yet \emph{relative} pathway-mass decay is nearly identical (at $f{=}0.5$, $k{=}8$: 0.3\% vs.\ 0.4\% of baseline; Fig.~\ref{fig:topology}a), while output degrades far more slowly (76.5\% at $f{=}0.5$ against 5.8\% four-hop route mass), ruling out graph redundancy alone. A candidate account is threshold amplification: with $N$ converging afferents and per-synapse survival $p$, aggregate drive scales as $Np$ while $k$-hop route mass decays as $p^k$ in expectation (6.25\% at $k{=}4$, $p{=}0.5$), and the saturating threshold compresses drive changes into small rate changes. Retention above proportional scaling (76.5\% at $p{=}0.5$, 89.2\% at $p{=}0.75$) is consistent with this account but does not establish it: in an E/I network even linear signal flow need not scale with $p$, since deleting inhibitory connections offsets excitatory loss. Disentangling these requires E/I-balanced deletion and drive-strength sweeps.

\subsection{From Transmission to Behavior: A Closed-Loop Escape Demo}
Transmission retention need not imply task capability, so we close the loop: looming rates over the 314 LC4/LPLC2 cells (assumed receptive fields) drive the full network, a frozen left--right difference across the eight command cells steers a planar agent away from a pursuing threat, and a ballistic takeoff fires when the 8-cell mean rate crosses $\theta_{\mathrm{demo}}{=}25.6$\,Hz (the median peak of six two-azimuth training episodes over 250--300\,ms; the open-loop $\theta{=}51.4$\,Hz was calibrated for full-pool drive and does not transfer). It escapes 24/24 across four azimuths ($\pm30^\circ$, $\pm60^\circ$; six seeds; median miss 0.09--0.11\,m against a 0.05\,m contact radius; Fig.~\ref{fig:demo}), including 12/12 at the two untrained azimuths ($+30^\circ$, $+60^\circ$), and 0/3 for a frontal approach (blind spot). Every control collapses (0/12 for steering disabled, readout disabled, and reversed sides), while a scene-reading hand rule reaches 12/12. Faults transfer as a threshold cliff rather than a graded rate loss: 25\% loss escapes 10/12 and 50\% escapes 0/12, although the same 50\% loss retains $72.4$\,Hz open-loop. Under single-target drive the F2$_{50}$ readout peaks at ${\approx}38$\,Hz and first reaches $\theta_{\mathrm{demo}}$ only at 420--500\,ms, when the threat is 7--11\,cm away---too late for the 250\,ms aiming delay and 450\,ms takeoff---so preserved transmission does not imply preserved behavior and the open-loop criterion is the wrong certificate for a closed loop. Hub silencing, by contrast, preserves escape (12/12) at $1.7\times$ healthy activity, inside the $2\times$ envelope. This is a preliminary synthetic demonstration, not a behavioral validation (assumed encoding, scripted envelope, kinematic agent).

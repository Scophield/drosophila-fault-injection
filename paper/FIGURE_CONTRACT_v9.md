# Figure contract — Python/matplotlib, IEEE final sizes

1. Overview (schematic-led composite): separate neural integration, feedback,
   actuation, early assessment and task clocks. Show common seed pairing without
   implying identical sensory histories. Schematic only; no invented data.
2. Independent outcomes (quantitative grid): every law/direction/dose has its
   denominator; show complete seed-cluster loss/gain evidence and no hidden floor.
3. Exposure and warning comparison (quantitative grid): distinguish stopped,
   common-duration, fixed-prefix and full-horizon estimands. Warn that the
   full-horizon resource trace continues after contact. Compare the deadline
   candidate with transmission, a calibrated TWO-SIDED activity rule, and an
   actuator-schedule baseline that sets future steering to zero from the same state;
   upper resource excursions alone are not a fair generic task detector.

Single-column width ~3.5 in or double-column ~7.16 in. White background;
restrained blue/orange/neutral colors plus marker/line-style distinctions.
Eight-point plot labels at final size, embedded TrueType (pdf.fonttype=42),
editable SVG and PDF, PNG previews. Every quantitative panel is generated from
saved analysis CSV/JSON with script/input hashes. Plot all pairs or report
explicit aggregation. Intervals are 20,000 complete-cluster percentile resamples
(seed 20261022), never dispersion bars or independent-episode confidence limits.
Final QA checks label collisions, axes, panel mapping, denominator/statistic
definitions, data hashes, all PDF pages, fonts, and print legibility.

Final current figures are generated into `audit/acceptance-v9/figures-v3/`.
The third layout version explicitly routes the feedback return above its label
to avoid a collision at single-column print size; quantitative values and
statistical definitions are unchanged. Previous figure versions and their
generating source snapshots remain available. The main presents raw outcome
denominators, while supplementary assessment intervals are cluster confidence
intervals; neither is a 10th--90th percentile dispersion bar.
